The transition from EVM to zkVM is not incremental — it is a regime change. Here's why we believe every major chain will migrate within five years, and what it means for developers building today.

## The EVM's Original Sin

The Ethereum Virtual Machine was designed for correctness and determinism, not for provability. Every opcode was chosen without considering whether a ZK proof could be generated over it efficiently. KECCAK256 is one of the most expensive operations to prove in a circuit — yet it's used everywhere in Ethereum's state trie.

zkVMs solve this by either redesigning the instruction set or by building massively parallelized proving infrastructure that can handle the EVM's idiosyncrasies at scale. Both approaches are valid; the winner will be determined by developer adoption, not technical elegance.

## Why This Time Is Different

Previous generations of ZK technology required hand-writing circuits for every computation — a process that took months per application. Modern zkVMs like SP1, RISC Zero, and Jolt allow any program compiled to RISC-V or WASM to be proven automatically. The proving overhead has fallen from 10,000× to under 100× in three years, and it's still falling.

At Moonshot Forge, we've deployed capital into three zkVM projects in the last 18 months. Our bet: by 2027, every new L1 and major L2 will be ZK-native.

## What This Means For Builders

If you're building a new protocol today, you have a choice: build on the EVM and accept the proving overhead, or build ZK-native and accept the smaller ecosystem. We believe the calculus is shifting rapidly toward ZK-native.

The tooling gap is closing fast. Solidity developers can already deploy to zkVMs with minimal changes. Within two years, the developer experience will be indistinguishable — but the performance and privacy guarantees will be dramatically better.

We're actively investing in three layers of the zkVM stack:

- **Proving infrastructure**: The hardware and software that generates proofs efficiently
- **Developer tooling**: Compilers, debuggers, and testing frameworks for ZK-native development
- **Application layer**: Protocols that leverage ZK properties for features impossible on traditional VMs

NullGate, our portfolio company, is building at the intersection of all three — a full-stack zkVM with privacy-preserving smart contracts and EVM compatibility at the opcode level.
