AI agents need three things to operate autonomously: money, memory, and verifiable compute. Today, all three are broken. We explain our thesis for why blockchain is the missing infrastructure layer.

## The Problem With Centralized AI

Current AI systems operate within walled gardens. An agent running on one provider's infrastructure cannot trustlessly transact with an agent running on another's. There is no standard for agent-to-agent payment, no verifiable record of what an agent has done, and no way to audit whether an AI actually executed the computation it claims.

Blockchain solves all three. On-chain wallets give agents a persistent, self-sovereign identity and payment rail. Smart contracts provide verifiable, auditable execution records. Decentralized compute networks ensure that inference results can be cryptographically proven.

## The Three Pillars

### Money
Agents need to transact autonomously. They need to pay for compute, buy data, and receive payment for services — all without human intervention. Crypto wallets with programmable spending policies are the natural solution. An agent can have its own wallet, governed by smart contract rules that limit spending and require multi-sig for large transactions.

### Memory
Today's AI agents are stateless between sessions. On-chain storage primitives give agents persistent, verifiable memory. An agent's history, reputation, and learned preferences can live on-chain, portable across any platform.

### Verifiable Compute
When an agent claims it ran a particular model on particular inputs, how do you verify that? Decentralized inference networks with proof-of-computation provide cryptographic guarantees that the computation actually happened as claimed.

We believe AI and crypto are not just complementary — they are co-dependent. AI needs crypto for trustless economic infrastructure. Crypto needs AI for automation and intelligence at the protocol level. The projects building at this intersection will define the next decade of both industries.
